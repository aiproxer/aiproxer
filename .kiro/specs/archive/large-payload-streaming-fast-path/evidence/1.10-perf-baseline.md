### 4.4 Call clone amplification (`lipapi.CloneCall` on the decoded Responses call)

| Size | ns/op | allocs/op | B/op | gc-cycles/op | live-heap-B | peak-heap-B |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 32KiB | 1410 | 7 | 1168 | 0 | 837320 | 1687552 |
| 256KiB | 610.0 | 7 | 1168 | 0 | 2256344 | 3080192 |
| 1MiB | 830.0 | 7 | 1168 | 0 | 5059976 | 5865472 |
| 5MiB | 750.0 | 7 | 1168 | 0 | 22623896 | 23429120 |

B/op and allocs/op are FLAT for this single-giant-string shape: cloning
copies the message/part slice headers and the string header, not the 5 MiB
string backing bytes. The ns/op jitter (610–1410 ns at only 10 iterations) is
10x timer noise on a sub-microsecond op; B/op is the stable signal. Clone
amplification for this shape is therefore RETENTION (the full `Call` tree,
including the 5 MiB string, stays alive across attempt derivation, baselines,
checkpoints — see Task 1.8 census), not per-clone byte duplication. Chunked
shapes with N messages/tools would scale B/op with N; the fast-path heap gate
(design 15) must gate on retention, not just `CloneCall` B/op.

## 5. What the baseline proves for design 15

- What should disappear on accepted wire requests (design 15) is all present
  and measured today: whole-body `[]byte` retention (`reqbody.ReadAll`),
  full DTO decode (4.2–4.3), `lipapi.Call` tree proportional to prompt size
  (4.2–4.3), `CloneCall` retention of that tree (4.4 + Task 1.8 census),
  provider re-serialization (4.5), and Call-retaining metering checkpoints
  (Task 1.8 census; not re-run here).
- What still exists (design 15) is also measured: provider upload from replay
  is transport-bound and heap-flat (4.6/4.8 ProviderOpen), while canonical
  validation/proof CPU (4.1–4.3) remains. V1 still receives/validates the full
  request before provider open; the gain is heap/GC plus avoided
  canonical-object/marshal/clone work, not early TTFT (Req 21.8).
- Heap invariant target (design 15): accepted spill-backed heap must be
  bounded by `memory_spool_bytes + max_semantic_fact_bytes + O(fixed
  buffers) + bounded metadata`, with 1 MiB → 5 MiB → 20 MiB approximately
  flat. Today canonical is linear (preflight 4.2x, decode 3.0x, pipe 15x);
  any future accepted-lane claim must be diffed against sections 4.1–4.8,
  not against pre-#592/#602 or #531-era numbers.
- Static-blocker benchmark (design 15, Req 21.12) is NOT included here; it
  belongs to Task 3.7 (definitely-canonical disposition vs disabled baseline
  with no spool/scanner/profile). This baseline is the disabled/current
  canonical side of that future comparison.

## 6. Eligibility and limits of this baseline

- Decode permits are never held during client upload today: `TryAdmit` runs
  after `reqbody.ReadAll` + shared preflight (`pipe.go:226-259`), and `Guard`
  holds the permit only across `RouteFromBodyModel`/decode (`268-284`). No
  assessment hold exists yet.
- At least one normal secure-session + metering production-like composition
  reaching wire execution (Req 21.6) cannot be shown by a baseline: there is
  no wire lane yet. Section 4.7 is frontend + mock-executor composition only;
  the secure-session/metering/billing blockers are inventoried in Task 1.8
  census and must be closed in Tasks 9–11 before Task 19.6 certification.
- 20 MiB single-string end-to-end 200 is not claimed: canonical `Validate`
  caps one text part at 8 MiB (`pkg/lipapi/items.go:177`), so a 20 MiB
  single-string body is a decode/preflight/clone/encode baseline only. A
  future 20 MiB 200 composition would need a chunked valid shape; Task 19.2
  owns giant-string/late-model/tools/malformed/failover cases.

## 7. Machine/mode caveats (do not compare across runners without re-running)

- Runner: Windows 11, AMD Ryzen 7 5800X 8-Core, `go1.26.6 windows/amd64`,
  no `-race` (Windows cgo toolchain limitation, same policy as Tasks 1.2–1.9).
- `GOMAXPROCS` default (16 threads reported as `-16` in benchmark names);
  benchmarks are sequential (no `RunParallel`) except the reused limiter
  parallel case; server is `httptest` loopback (includes TCP/stack, not WAN).
- `-benchtime=10x` throughout to keep CI bounded: cheap ops (`CloneCall`,
  limiter) are timer-noisy at 10 iterations (see 4.4 jitter); B/op and
  allocs/op are the stable signals there. Throughput (`MB/s`) uses
  `SetBytes(len(body))`.
- `live-heap-B`/`peak-heap-B` are process-absolute `HeapAlloc`/`HeapInuse`
  after each sub-benchmark (body + harness retained); deltas and slopes
  across sizes are the comparable signal, not the absolute bytes.
- `gc-pause-ns/op` reads `PauseTotalNs` delta; zeros at 10x mean no STW pause
  was sampled in those 10 iterations, not zero GC cost (`gc-cycles/op` and
  B/op carry the cost).
